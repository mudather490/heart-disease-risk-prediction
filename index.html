🧠 How Logistic Regression Works (Step-by-Step)

Demystifying the underlying linear algebra and probability mapping

Step 1

Feature Standardization

\( x_{scaled} = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma} \)

Features are centered around mean \(\mu\) and scaled by standard deviation \(\sigma\) so large numerical ranges (e.g. cholesterol) don't overpower small ones (e.g. sex).

Step 2

Linear Combination (Z-Score)

\( z = \beta_0 + \sum_{i=1}^{n} \beta_i x_{i, scaled} \)

The model multiplies each scaled feature by its learned weight (\(\beta_i\)) and adds the intercept (\(\beta_0 = --\)).

Step 3

Sigmoid Transformation

\( p = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}} \)

Squeezes the unbounded Z-score (which can range from \(-\infty\) to \(+\infty\)) into a valid probability between 0.0 (0%) and 1.0 (100%).

⚖️ Addressing Class Imbalance & Model Performance

Why standard accuracy can be deceiving in medical diagnosis

💡 Framingham Dataset Insight: Out of 3,656 clean patients, 3,099 (84.8%) had No Risk and only 557 (15.2%) had Heart Disease Risk. A baseline model predicting "No Risk" for everyone achieves ~84.8% accuracy but catches 0% of sick patients!
